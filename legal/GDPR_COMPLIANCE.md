## 2. Lawful Basis for Processing

### 2.1 Article 6 - Lawful Basis for Processing Personal Data

**Contract Performance (Article 6(1)(b)):**
- Creating and managing user accounts
- Providing content protection services
- Processing payments and subscriptions
- Delivering AI-powered insights and analytics
- Customer support and technical assistance

**Legitimate Interests (Article 6(1)(f)):**
- Platform security and fraud prevention
- Service improvement and development
- Business analytics and research
- Direct marketing to existing customers
- Legal claim defense and establishment

**Legal Obligation (Article 6(1)(c)):**
- Tax reporting and financial compliance
- Regulatory reporting requirements
- Court orders and legal process compliance
- Anti-money laundering (AML) obligations

**Consent (Article 6(1)(a)):**
- Marketing communications to prospects
- Cookies and tracking technologies
- Special category data processing
- Data transfers to third countries without adequacy decisions

### 2.2 Article 9 - Special Categories of Personal Data
We generally do not process special categories of personal data. Where necessary:
- **Explicit Consent:** Required for any special category processing
- **Vital Interests:** Only in exceptional circumstances for data subject protection
- **Legal Claims:** For establishment, exercise, or defense of legal claims

## 3. Data Subject Rights Implementation

### 3.1 Right of Access (Article 15)
**Implementation:**
- Self-service portal in user account dashboard
- Downloadable personal data export functionality
- Response within 1 month (extendable by 2 months for complex requests)
- No charge for initial request; reasonable fee for excessive requests

**Information Provided:**
- Purposes of processing
- Categories of personal data
- Recipients or categories of recipients
- Retention periods
- Rights available to the data subject
- Source of data (if not collected directly)

### 3.2 Right to Rectification (Article 16)
**Implementation:**
- Real-time correction through account settings
- Automated validation for data accuracy
- Notification to recipients of corrected data
- Documentation of all corrections made

### 3.3 Right to Erasure ("Right to be Forgotten") (Article 17)
**Implementation:**
- Automated deletion process through account settings
- Complete removal from all systems within 30 days
- Notification to data processors and recipients
- Exceptions documented for legal retention requirements

**Exceptions Applied:**
- Legal obligation compliance
- Public interest archiving
- Freedom of expression and information
- Legal claim establishment, exercise, or defense

### 3.4 Right to Restriction of Processing (Article 18)
**Implementation:**
- Temporary suspension of processing activities
- Data marking and isolation procedures
- Limited processing only for storage and legal claims
- Notification before lifting restrictions

### 3.5 Right to Data Portability (Article 20)
**Implementation:**
- Machine-readable format export (JSON, CSV, XML)
- Direct transfer capability to other controllers (where technically feasible)
- Comprehensive data package including all personal data
- Secure transfer protocols and authentication

### 3.6 Right to Object (Article 21)
**Implementation:**
- Opt-out mechanisms for direct marketing
- Objection handling for legitimate interests processing
- Immediate cessation unless compelling legitimate grounds
- Clear information about objection rights

### 3.7 Rights Related to Automated Decision-Making (Article 22)
**Implementation:**
- Clear disclosure of automated decision-making use
- Right to human intervention and review
- Right to express point of view and contest decisions
- Safeguards for significant automated decisions

## 4. Privacy by Design and by Default (Article 25)

### 4.1 Technical Measures
**Data Minimization:**
- Collection limited to necessary data only
- Purpose limitation enforced through system design
- Regular data inventory and cleanup processes
- Automated retention policy enforcement

**Security by Design:**
- End-to-end encryption for data in transit (TLS 1.3)
- AES-256 encryption for data at rest
- Multi-factor authentication requirements
- Regular security assessments and penetration testing

**Privacy-Preserving Technologies:**
- Pseudonymization and anonymization techniques
- Differential privacy for analytics
- Homomorphic encryption for secure computation
- Zero-knowledge proof implementations where applicable

### 4.2 Organizational Measures
**Privacy Impact Assessments (PIAs):**
- Mandatory for high-risk processing activities
- Regular review and update schedule
- Stakeholder consultation process
- Documentation and approval workflow

**Data Protection by Default:**
- Most privacy-friendly settings as default
- Granular consent mechanisms
- Clear and accessible privacy controls
- Regular review of default settings

## 7. Data Breach Management (Articles 33-34)

### 7.1 Breach Detection and Assessment
**Automated Monitoring:**
- 24/7 security monitoring and alerting
- Anomaly detection for unusual data access
- System integrity monitoring
- Regular vulnerability assessments

**Risk Assessment Criteria:**
- Type and volume of data involved
- Number of data subjects affected
- Likelihood of harm to data subjects
- Ability to mitigate risks and consequences

### 7.2 Breach Notification Procedures
**Supervisory Authority Notification (Article 33):**
- Notification within 72 hours of becoming aware
- Phased notification approach for ongoing investigations
- Comprehensive documentation and evidence
- Follow-up reports as investigation progresses

**Data Subject Notification (Article 34):**
- Direct notification for high-risk breaches
- Clear, plain language communications
- Mitigation measures and recommendations
- Contact information for further inquiries

### 7.3 Breach Response Plan
**Immediate Response (0-24 hours):**
1. Incident detection and initial assessment
2. Containment and evidence preservation
3. Initial risk assessment
4. Internal stakeholder notification

**Short-term Response (24-72 hours):**
1. Detailed investigation and analysis
2. Supervisory authority notification (if required)
3. Breach registry documentation
4. Communication strategy development

**Long-term Response (72+ hours):**
1. Data subject notification (if required)
2. Remediation and prevention measures
3. Lessons learned and process improvement
4. Regular monitoring and follow-up
