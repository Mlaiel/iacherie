### 3.3 Filing a DMCA Takedown Notice
To file a valid DMCA takedown notice, please provide:

**Required Information:**
1. **Identification of copyrighted work:** Describe the copyrighted work you claim has been infringed
2. **Identification of infringing material:** Provide specific location/URL of the allegedly infringing material
3. **Contact information:** Your name, address, phone number, and email address
4. **Good faith statement:** A statement that you have a good faith belief that the use is not authorized
5. **Accuracy statement:** A statement that the information is accurate and you are authorized to act
6. **Signature:** Physical or electronic signature of the copyright owner or authorized agent

**Notice Format:**
```
Subject: DMCA Takedown Notice - [Description of Work]

Dear DMCA Agent,

I am writing to report copyright infringement of my work on [Platform Name].

1. COPYRIGHTED WORK:
   Title: [Work Title]
   Description: [Brief description]
   Copyright Registration: [If applicable]
   Original Publication: [Date and location]

2. INFRINGING MATERIAL:
   URL: [Specific URL of infringing content]
   Description: [How the content infringes]
   Date Discovered: [Date]

3. CONTACT INFORMATION:
   Name: [Your Full Name]
   Address: [Complete Address]
   Phone: [Phone Number]
   Email: [Email Address]

4. STATEMENTS:
   I have a good faith belief that the use of the material described above is not authorized by the copyright owner, its agent, or the law.
   
   I swear, under penalty of perjury, that the information in this notification is accurate and that I am the copyright owner or am authorized to act on behalf of the owner.

5. SIGNATURE:
   [Electronic or Physical Signature]
   Date: [Date]
```

### 3.4 DMCA Counter-Notification Process
If you believe your content was removed in error, you may file a counter-notification:

**Counter-Notification Requirements:**
1. **Identification:** Identify the material that was removed and its location
2. **Good faith statement:** Statement that removal was due to mistake or misidentification
3. **Consent to jurisdiction:** Consent to federal court jurisdiction in your district
4. **Contact information:** Your name, address, and phone number
5. **Signature:** Physical or electronic signature

**Processing Counter-Notifications:**
- We will forward valid counter-notifications to the original complainant
- Material may be restored in 10-14 business days unless complainant files court action
- We reserve the right to evaluate counter-notifications for validity

## 4. International Copyright Compliance

### 4.1 European Union - Copyright Directive
**EU Copyright Directive (2019/790) Compliance:**
- Article 17 upload filter requirements for user-generated content platforms
- Licensing negotiations with rights holders
- Exception for legitimate uses (parody, criticism, review)
- Complaint and redress mechanisms for users

### 4.2 United Kingdom - Copyright, Designs and Patents Act
**UK Copyright Compliance:**
- Recognition of moral rights and economic rights
- Fair dealing exceptions for research, private study, criticism, and review
- Notice and takedown procedures aligned with UK law
- Cooperation with UK Intellectual Property Office

### 4.3 Canada - Copyright Modernization Act
**Canadian Copyright Compliance:**
- Notice and notice system (not notice and takedown)
- Safe harbor provisions for online intermediaries
- Fair dealing exceptions for education, parody, and satire
- Compliance with Copyright Board of Canada decisions

### 4.4 Australia - Copyright Act 1968
**Australian Copyright Compliance:**
- Safe harbor scheme for carriage service providers
- Fair dealing exceptions for research, study, criticism, and review
- Industry codes of practice for copyright enforcement
- Cooperation with Australian Communications and Media Authority

### 4.5 Other Jurisdictions

- **Germany:** Urheberrechtsgesetz (UrhG)
- **France:** Code de la propriété intellectuelle

## 5. Content Licensing and Fair Use

### 5.1 Licensed Content

### 5.2 Fair Use Guidelines (US)
We consider fair use factors when evaluating copyright claims:
- **Purpose and character:** Commercial vs. educational/transformative use
- **Nature of work:** Factual vs. creative content
- **Amount used:** Portion of work used relative to the whole
- **Market effect:** Impact on the market for the original work
